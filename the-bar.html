From horse float to cocktail bar.

It used to carry horses. We stripped it back and rebuilt it as a bar, here in Palmerston.

The conversion

When we bought the trailer it was a retired horse float with a sound frame and not much else going for it.

We took it back to bare metal, cut a serving hatch into the side, lined the inside with timber and painted the outside matte black. It now has cold storage, a full-length bar top and enough room for the bartenders to work without bumping elbows.

The name comes from the trailer’s first job. A trough is where you water the horses. Ours just pours something better.

Why we pre-batch

A cocktail made from scratch takes a couple of minutes. With a hundred guests and one bar, that turns into a queue.

We mix and measure each cocktail before service starts, then pour and garnish to order.

  • Speed. A finished drink takes seconds.
  • Consistency. Each batch is measured once, so the last drink tastes like the first.
  • Less queueing. Guests get back to the party sooner.

The team

Every bartender on our team holds a current Responsible Service of Alcohol (RSA) certificate.

They serve responsibly, keep an eye on the room and can refuse service when that’s the right call.

We arrive about 45 minutes before service to set up and stay about 45 minutes after to pack down. Both are included in every booking.
